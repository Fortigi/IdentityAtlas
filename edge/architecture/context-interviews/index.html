    Context interviews — what people say, as claims¶

    Status: built (first slice), on organisational units and logical applications.

    An analyst talks to people about a customer, a team or an application. What they say is often the only source for facts no crawler has: what the unit does, since when it is a customer, who really does the work. Add information on a context's page takes any source that says something about it — the transcript of such a conversation, notes, an Excel or CSV list, a Word document, or pasted text — with who it is from (optional) and when, and turns what it says into claims — each a statement with the verbatim words it rests on — for an analyst to review. Nothing is written to the context until someone confirms it.

    "Interview" in the API and the tables means any added source. The records are ContextInterviews rows and the paths are /contexts/:id/interviews…, kept as they were; the page calls them Sources. A row's kind (sourceKind) is read from its columns, not stored: interview (it names who said it), file (nobody in particular: a list, a document — intervieweeName then holds who added it) or text (pasted, stored under the file name Pasted text). See Sources and Add information.

    Example: "Yes, I'm the owner of this app, but it's mostly Piet who does the day-to-day" suggests the interviewee as the application's Accountable (its Owner) and Piet as Responsible, in the People block next to the existing best guess.

    The one decision: a claim is not a fact¶

    The interview is a source; a claim is what that source said about a subject. The two live in their own tables (ContextInterviews, ContextClaims), apart from the graph, and nothing crosses into the graph without a person's decision. In the ontology (2.2.0) they are the knowledge layer: ia:Interview (a subclass of ia:Source) and ia:Claim, both under ia:Knowledge — not nodes, not edges, and never a way of holding access (the holding rule is untouched).

    A confirmed claim of kind Changes
    description appended to the context's description under From interview with NAME, DATE: (a second description from the same interview goes under the same heading)
    property extendedAttributes[attribute] = value
    role with a roleSuggestion the ContextRole — addedBy = 'analyst' (a person decided), sourceClaimId = the claim
    alias added to extendedAttributes.aliases (an array; a spelling already there — case, accents, dots aside — is not added twice), shown as Also known as
    role without one, roleHistory, membership nothing beyond the claim: confirmed knowledge, shown on the person's row in the People overview. A membership claim never changes ContextMembers — members come from their source

    Accountable. Set only when the context has none, or the same person already holds it. Otherwise the API answers 409 with the current holder and the page asks "X is the Accountable now. Replace them with Y?"; confirming again with replace: true replaces them. A Responsible is added next to the others.

    Provenance on roles. A role confirmed from an interview keeps addedBy = 'analyst' — so the plugin runner and the synced-owner reconcile leave it alone, exactly as every analyst role — and records its claim in the new nullable ContextRoles.sourceClaimId. The roles API returns the interviewee, the date and the quote with it, and the People block says "from the interview with …". No new addedBy value was added: who decided is the analyst; where it came from is the claim.

    Data (migrations 099, 100, 101)¶

    ContextInterviews — contextId (cascade), the interviewee as principalId or identityId (at most one; set NULL when that row goes) and intervieweeName (kept), interviewedAt (date), fileName, transcript (plain text, at most 5,000,000 characters), language, extractionStatus (pending, running, done, failed, notConfigured), extractionMessage, extractionStartedAt, model, createdBy, createdAt.

    ContextClaims — interviewId (cascade), contextId, claimKind (description, property, role, roleHistory, membership, alias), subjectKind (Context | Person), the person as principalId | identityId | neither, plus subjectName as spoken and subjectMatch (interviewee, key, name, fuzzy, firstName), attribute (a property's key, a role claim's relation: owner, notOwner, dayToDay, delegate, member, other — or a membership claim's status: current, former, joined, left, rejoined — a closed list in code (claims.js MEMBERSHIP_STATUSES) and the ontology, not a CHECK, so rejoined needed no migration), value, periodFrom / periodTo (dates) and periodText (as said), quote, confidence (0–1, not a decision), relevance (high | low; NULL, from before 100, reads as high), quoteStart / quoteEnd (the quote's span in the stored transcript, string offsets, end exclusive), roleSuggestion, claimStatus (proposed, confirmed, edited, rejected), proposedBy (model | analyst), decidedBy, decidedAt. Every change is recorded in _history.

    Migration 100 widened the kinds and added relevance, quoteStart and quoteEnd. It is a separate migration rather than an edit of 099 because the demo database had already run 099 (and a changed migration fails the hash ratchet).

    ContextMemberOverrides (101) — a member marked former by a decision, on top of ContextMembers: contextId (cascade), memberId, claimId (cascade), overrides (the source's name when decided), decidedBy, decidedAt; one per (context, member). See Marked former by a decision under Contradictions.

    (claimStatus and proposedBy are not called status and origin because the ontology names a column property after its column, and ia:status / ia:origin already mean something else. The API still says status.)

    Reading a transcript¶

    .txt (UTF-8, or UTF-16 with a BOM), .docx (the paragraphs of word/document.xml), .vtt and .srt (cue ids, timings and notes dropped; a speaker's consecutive cues joined into one turn, "Name: words"). At most 25 MB per file.

    Extraction — with the local model¶

    When the report generator's model server is configured (NL_REPORTS_LLM_URL, the one the list upload's refinement uses), the upload starts extraction in the background:

    Where the text goes. Extraction sends the transcript's text — names, roles and whatever else was said — to the model server at NL_REPORTS_LLM_URL. The shipped nl-reports overlay runs that server in your own Docker stack, so nothing leaves it. Point the variable at a hosted model and the transcripts go there instead; leave it unset and no transcript is sent anywhere — it is stored and you add claims by hand (Without a model).

    1. The transcript is read in parts of 10,000 characters, at most 40 (a longer one says how much was read — never silently less). Each part is asked for claims with a prompt that names what identity and access governance runs on — people and their roles and membership over time (who joined, who left, who worked on it before), who owns it or answers for it, other names it goes by, the systems used, facts that change who may have access (sector, contract, regulation, sensitive data) — and says to skip small talk and generic facts; then what differs per template (a unit: what it is, customer since, who leads it; an application: purpose, users, data, vendor, owner, day-to-day people). A strict JSON schema, with a relevance tier per claim; an unreadable reply gets one repair round. Each answer may take the report model's time budget — INTERVIEW_LLM_TIMEOUT_MS, else NL_REPORTS_LLM_TIMEOUT_MS (the nl-reports overlay sets 900000), else 15 minutes. Extraction runs in the background, and on a 2-CPU box with a 4B model one part takes minutes — longer when the model server's single slot is still busy with another request (a report, a prompt-cache warm-up): the request simply waits. The model's own HTTP client (nlreports/llamacpp.js, ollama.js) stops at NL_REPORTS_LLM_TIMEOUT_MS, so a larger interview budget does not outlast it.
    2. Every claim is checked: closed kinds, a value, a person for a role claim, an attribute for a property — and the quote must be a verbatim part of the transcript (compared after folding whitespace and typographic quotes), or the claim is dropped. A near-verbatim quote — only case, punctuation or spacing differ — is kept, with the transcript's own words as its quote; different words never match. What a small model leaves out is filled where that needs no guess: a property without an attribute name becomes a description claim, a claim without a value takes its quote as the value, a roleHistory without a period is kept with the period empty. A role without its person, an unknown kind and a quote that is not there are still dropped. Every drop is logged with the field it failed on, and the run's message says it — "2 incomplete claims were left out (1 without the person, 1 of an unknown kind). 1 claim had a field filled in from the quote — check it." There is no second model round asking for the missing fields: on a small box it costs as much as the part itself.
    3. A person claim whose subject joins two or more names ("Ruud en Kees", "Ruud and Kees", "A, B & C") becomes one claim per name (roles.js splitJointSubjects; en / and are no name particles, so the quote's-full-name fallback never glues them either). A claim that says someone left is read against its whole sentence (interviews/sentences.js): a small model quotes a fragment and decides from it — "Gerrit is sinds iets meer dan een jaar betrokken en doet data mining" came back as former. The quote's span is widened to the sentence(s) it stands in (cut at . ! ? and line breaks; a turn is one line and its Speaker: label is not part of it). For a membership former / left, and a role / roleHistory whose words say left:
    4. the sentence says gone (a Who left word, or past-only phrasing: een periode, destijds, vroeger, used to, for a while) → kept — "Stijn en Noor hebben een periode meegewerkt, maar zijn inmiddels uitgestroomd" stays former even when the model quoted only the first half;
    5. else it says current (sinds, is betrokken, doet, nu, currently, now, works) → a membership becomes current, and its value says so: "… (corrected: the sentence says current; the model said former)" — the review shows it, and the analyst can edit or reject it; a role claim keeps its relation but loses the left reading (no former membership is derived from it; leftSignal.js reads the same mark);
    6. neither → kept, its confidence lowered to at most 0.3; the review flags a claim below 0.4 unsure — read the quote.

    Before all three: the sentence says the person came back (leftSignal.js saysReturned: rejoined, came back, back as, returned, joined again; weer terug, teruggekomen, kwam terug, is terug, terug als / bij, opnieuw, weer bij, and "is sinds … terug") → a membership becomes rejoined, a role claim loses its left reading; the value says so ("corrected: the sentence says they came back"). A bare terug or weer is not enough — "een jaar terug" is "a year ago".

    The stored quote stays the transcript's own words; when the sentence is at most 300 characters it becomes the whole sentence, so the highlight shows the reason. Repeats are dropped, and so is anything already kept on this interview, and an "alias" that is the context's own name or an alias it already has. 4. Names are matched: the interviewee ("I", their name, their first name); then the upload's account matching (upload/members.js: e-mail, employee id, external id, the normalised name, a near miss — fuzzy); then a first name alone, firstName (interviews/firstNames.js), searched in widening circles — one statement per circle for all the names at once, and the first circle that decides wins: (a) the context's current members and role holders; (b) the people its interviews already name — the holders of its claims (any interview) and its interviewees, which is where a former member is found; (c) the members and role holders of the contexts next to it — its SameUnitAs variants, its parent and its siblings; (d) the whole organisation: a live user account, only when exactly one has that first name (20 or more candidate rows is no match — at a large organisation this rarely resolves, by design). Two people with the first name in a circle is no match, and the wider circles are not asked: they cannot make it unique. Two fallbacks for a name still unmatched, both fuzzy: - the quote's full name — a small model cuts names short ("Vries" for "Piet de Vries does most of the day-to-day work"). The quote is verbatim, so a longer run of 2–4 capitalised words in it (particles such as van / de / der / den / von / el between them) that contains the subject's words is tried, longest first; the first that resolves wins and the claim carries that name (interviews/nameSpan.js); - a unique surname — a surname alone ("Vries", "de Vries") that ends the name of exactly one live user account; two such accounts, or 200 or more candidate rows, is no match.

    fuzzy and firstName are flagged check the person. An unmatched name keeps its raw text.

    Decided claims do not come back. A claim equal to one already confirmed, edited or rejected on the context — in any of its interviews — is dropped: same kind, same person (the matched holder when both have one, else the normalised name) and the same value (case, punctuation and spacing aside). A re-extraction, or a second interview, no longer re-proposes "chief architect" for an interviewee whose role claim was confirmed last week, nor a rejected claim.

    Another name the speaker uses (interviews/aliasHints.js). The prompt names the context's display name and its known aliases and asks for an alias claim when the speaker uses another name. A small model often still leaves it out, so a rule proposes one — an alias claim with confidence 0.5, waiting for review like any other: a name after het / de / the (up to four capitalised words; "Jan de Vries" is a surname — an article after a capitalised word is skipped), or the name a description claim starts with ("Het Havenkantoor is …"), said at least twice, or in the quote of the first description claim. Never the context's own name or an alias it has (spacing ignored: Harbour Office is HarbourOffice), an alias the model gave, a pronoun, a word of a person's name (any person claim, the interviewee), a name a property claim gives ("the Azure platform"), or another context's display name or alias (store.otherContextNames, normalised as lib/nameMatch.js does). At most three per run; the quote is the sentence of the first occurrence. 5. Relevance (contexts/interviews/relevance.js, fixed rules first): role, roleHistory, membership and alias are always high; a property is high when its attribute or value names something access depends on (sector, contract, customer, regulation, systems, applications, data, vendor, hosting, …), else the model's tier, else low; a description takes the model's tier, else high only when it names such a term. Each claim also gets the span of its quote in the transcript (quoteOffsets.js). 6. Fixed rules (contexts/interviews/roles.js, no model) set the role suggestions: owner → Accountable, dayToDay / delegate → Responsible, notOwner → none. A subject who says both that they own it and that they do not gets no Accountable suggestion. "I'm not the owner, Y is" is two claims: notOwner for the interviewee, owner for Y — and when the interviewee is the current Accountable, the People block shows In doubt: with the quote. A role / roleHistory claim that says the person left (see Who left below — "recently left", "inmiddels uitgestroomd") adds a membership claim with status former for the same person, quote and period, unless the model already gave a membership claim for that person (one per person; a re-run does not propose one already decided). 7. The interview's undecided model claims are replaced in one transaction; status done. Any failure: status failed with the reason, and Read again.

    The model only proposes (decision principle B3): the confirmed claim is the record.

    Without a model — never a dead end¶

    The transcript is stored with status notConfigured, and the interview list offers Add claim: the transcript read-only beside a small form; select words and Use the selection as quote (checked as verbatim by the API). The claim passes the same check as a model's, its person is matched the same way, and a role claim's suggestion is deduced from its relation unless the analyst picks one.

    API¶

    All writes need data.write.contexts; OpenAPI documents each.

    POST   /api/contexts/:id/interviews                       multipart file (or text) + principalId|identityId (optional) + interviewedAt
GET    /api/contexts/:id/interviews                       the interviews, with claim counts (writers)
GET    /api/contexts/:id/interviews/:interviewId          one, with its transcript and highlights [{ claimId, start, end }] (writers)
POST   /api/contexts/:id/interviews/:interviewId/extract  read again (409 while a run of < 30 min is going; 503 without a model)
POST   /api/contexts/:id/interviews/:interviewId/claims   an analyst's own claim
DELETE /api/contexts/:id/interviews/:interviewId          the interview and its claims
GET    /api/contexts/:id/claims?status=…                  the claims + accountableDoubt
POST   /api/contexts/:id/claims/reject                    { kind?, relevance? }: reject the proposed claims of a kind / tier, one statement
GET    /api/contexts/:id/people?filter=&search=&limit=&offset=   the People overview: one row per person (every reader)
GET    /api/contexts/:id/members                          (existing) the members as their source lists them
PATCH  /api/contexts/claims/:claimId                      { status: confirmed|edited|rejected, value?, period?, principalId|identityId?, replace? }
GET    /api/contexts/claims/mentions?principalId=|identityId=   the contexts whose interviews mention a person

    Deleting an interview removes its claims. What its confirmed claims already changed — a description section, an attribute, a role — stays; a role only loses its link (sourceClaimId → NULL).

    Sources and Add information¶

    Add information (in the page header and on the Sources section, analysts only) opens one dialog — "A transcript, notes, an Excel or CSV list, a Word document — anything that says something about this unit." — with two ways in:

    • Upload a file: .txt, .md, .docx, .vtt, .srt are read as a transcript (above). .csv and .xlsx are read with the list upload's own reader (contexts/upload/parse.js parseList) and each row becomes one readable line, "Name: Ann Example; Role: Architect" (empty cells left out), so the model reads a list like text and a quote can be a row (contexts/interviews/sourceText.js). The original file name stays on the source.
    • Paste text: notes, an e-mail; stored under the file name Pasted text.

    Who is this from? is optional. With someone, the source is an interview and "I" in it is that person; without, the source is the file (or the text) and whoever added it — the model is told nobody in particular is speaking, and "I" names nobody. The date is the date of the source.

    Sources (folded to "2 sources · latest 2026-10-23") lists every added source: its kind (Interview, File, Pasted text), who it is from or who added it, when, the file, how far the model got, its claim counts, and View (the text with its quotes highlighted), Read again, Add claim (by hand) and Delete.

    The People overview¶

    One section for everyone in a unit and everyone who was — the members its source lists merged with what the added sources say about people. Each person appears once: GET /contexts/:id/people (contexts/interviews/people.js has the rules, peopleLoad.js the reads) keys a person by their holder in the context's own kind of member — the account for a unit of accounts, the person for a unit of people; a claim that names the other kind is mapped through IdentityMembers — and a name nobody matched by the name as said. Every row carries one status:

    Status When
    Current the source lists them
    Came back the source lists them, and a source says they left once and came back (rejoinOf); the row says "earlier: Junior developer"
    Not confirmed a source says they are in it, but the member list does not list them
    Left (decided) the source still lists them, but a decision marked them former (below)
    Left a source says they left, and the list does not list them (any more)

    Accountable and Responsible are badges on the row (from ContextRoles). The columns are compact: the name (opening their page), the status, their latest role here, where it comes from (list, interview, file, notes), what they still hold (live entitlements — a person's own and their accounts' — and how many of those are on this context's resources, "3 here"), and the claims about them as icons, one per kind (role, membership, history): outlined and dashed while a claim of that kind is proposed, solid once confirmed. Hovering or focusing an icon shows the claim in one line, the quote (cut at about 200 characters), its source and its status; an analyst gets Confirm / Reject there and Show in the source, and clicking the icon opens the source at that quote. A person in an open contradiction gets a warning icon whose popover shows both statements and the decision buttons (the Contradictions panel's own). Escape closes a popover.

    Filters: Current (current, came back, not confirmed) · Former (left (decided), left) · All, each with its count, and To decide while there are open contradictions about people. Sorted: the listed current members by name, then the rest by status, each by name. Paged at 100. The read is set-based — a fixed number of statements whatever the size of the unit: the listed current members are the only group read a page at a time (the members endpoint's own read); everyone the claims and decisions name is few and merged in memory. An application's members are resources: its page keeps the resources list and shows People only when a source named someone.

    Who left (contexts/interviews/leftSignal.js, one shared rule): a membership claim with status former or left, or a role / roleHistory claim (not notOwner) whose value or period says it — left, no longer, former(ly), quit, departed, moved on; uitgestroomd, vertrokken, weg, niet meer, gestopt, voormalig(e), oud-… — whole words only. Who came back (memberFacts.js rejoinOf): a person whose latest word does not say they left, and for whom a claim says they returned — a membership rejoined; the return words in a role claim's value or period, or in the clause of its quote about them; or a present membership said in a later interview than one that said they left. Their earlier stint is every role before the latest one. A promotion without a word for coming back is no second stint. Proposed claims read dashed and italic, confirmed ones plain. The reader rule is the claims list's: a reader gets confirmed claims only.

    Aliases¶

    A confirmed alias claim adds to extendedAttributes.aliases (lib/contextNameMatch.js, mergeAliases). On a unit or an application the profile's facts show Also known as (interviews/AlsoKnownAs.jsx, claims.helpers.js aliasFacts): the stored aliases and the confirmed alias claims plain, then — for an analyst — the proposed ones dashed, each with Confirm / Reject inline. (More details no longer repeats the attribute there.) Before this, a proposed alias was only in the review list, folded inside What we know among all the other claims — which is why it looked missing. After any claim decision the page re-reads the context quietly (useContextDetail refreshDetail: no loading state, open panels stay), so a confirmed alias, property or description shows at once. Anything that places a name onto a context should try aliases too: buildContextNameMatcher (same file) tries a context's display name, then an alias (exact, normalised), then a near miss of the display name — never a near miss of an alias, which is short. No caller in this branch matches context names: the list upload matches contexts by key, the report-script lookup is client-side. The activity upload (feature/context-activity) places rows by context name and is meant to adopt this matcher.

    Contradictions¶

    A claim that only adds knowledge keeps the ordinary review. A claim that disagrees with something we hold — or with another claim — is shown as a pair and asks for a decision (contexts/interviews/contradictions.js, fixed rules, set-based: the claims already read, one statement for the context and its source system, one for which of the people named are direct members). GET …/claims returns them as contradictions:

    Rule The claim Against
    leftButMember membership former / left, or a role claim that says left the member list still lists them ("Interview: Lotte left · HR export (upload) still lists Lotte") — not when they came back
    memberNotListed membership current / joined / rejoined the member list does not list them
    accountable a role claim suggesting X as Accountable Y is the Accountable (synced owner, a rule, an analyst)
    notOwner the Accountable says they are not the owner they are the Accountable — this is the old In doubt (accountableDoubt stays in the response for the People block)
    property a property claim the context's attribute holds another value (case, accents, spacing aside)
    claims two claims one person current in one interview and gone in another; two values for one property; two people named as owner while there is no Accountable

    Aliases never contradict: a context may have any number of names, and a name that is another context's is dropped at extraction. Each side carries its source: the claim its interviewee, date and quote; a fact ", last synced " (Systems.lastSyncDateTime, else "listed since" the member's addedAt; "The member list" for a context kept by hand).

    Deciding one is deciding its claim(s) — no table of its own. The claim's status, decidedBy and decidedAt are the record (and _history keeps the change): The interview is right confirms it (a member who left is marked former by the decision — see below; for another owner it confirms with replace); The list is right / Keep … rejects it; Both true, at different times saves it with the period asked for (edited); of a pair, keeping one confirms it and rejects the other, both confirms both. A decided contradiction says how (resolution: interview, source, both, first, second, neither), leaves the panel, and the Timeline shows "Decided: · — the interview is right" in place of the plain decision (a pair once, on its last decision). A rule that no longer holds after the decision (an Accountable replaced, an attribute set) stops matching; its decision reads as an ordinary one.

    Two interviews that disagree about a person are a contradiction while either claim is proposed; confirmed both (an earlier left, a later current), they are a return — rejoinOf counts them, so "Both true, at different times" on that pair makes the person rejoined.

    Marked former by a decision (migration 101)¶

    "Interview: Led is a former member" against "Klanten (upload) still lists Led as a member", decided the interview is right: Led is no longer a current member, but a former one. The decision is an override on top of ContextMembers, never a change to it — the source would add the row back on its next sync or re-import. ContextMemberOverrides holds one row per (context, member): the claim it was decided on (claimId, cascade: deleting the interview deletes the mark), the source it overrides by name (overrides), decidedBy, decidedAt; _history records it. contexts/interviews/memberOverrides.js is the only writer: applyMemberOverride (inside the decision's transaction) and clearClaimOverride (undo).

    Decision Claim Mark
    The interview is right confirmed (or edited without a period) set — when the source lists the person, by the context's own kind (an account of a unit of accounts, a person of a unit of people)
    The list is right rejected none: the member stays current
    Both true, at different times edited with the period none: the member stays current; the period is the earlier stint in the claim (the person's history icon in People)
    a claim that they are in it (current, joined, rejoined) confirmed confirmed cleared
    Undo decision (POST /contexts/claims/:id/undo) back to proposed removed; the contradiction is open again

    Undo is for a decision that changed nothing but the claim: any rejection, and a confirmed membership, roleHistory or role without a suggestion (409 otherwise — a description, property, alias or role is changed back where it was changed).

    What reads the mark:

    • Members list (GET …/members): a marked member is left out of the current members and of total; overriddenCount says how many were left out; ?includeOverridden=true lists them too, each with overridden: { by, at, overrides, claimId } (GET /contexts/:id → overriddenMemberCount; the counts stay the source's).
    • People overview (GET …/people): the person is Left (decided), under Former, with decision and stillListed and the live entitlements they still hold — the status says "Decided by , : the interview is right — Klanten (upload) still lists them. Still listed in the source — remove it there or re-import without them.", and the popover of the claim it was decided on offers Undo decision to an analyst.
    • Timeline: "Decided: Led Ledeboer is a former member (overrides Klanten (upload))".
    • Unit report: keeps the row and adds a Status column — current or marked former (decision) — so the report shows both what the source lists and what was decided.
    • Context owner (read-only view): the same People overview, from confirmed claims only — Former included: who left but can still get in is the most useful thing the page tells them.
    • Matrix and access: unchanged, deliberately. The matrix shows access, and the access is still there — that is the point of the People overview's Access column. The decision never removes an assignment or a membership.
    • Change request: when the source is a system or an uploaded list, the mark also raises a change request: "Remove Led Ledeboer from Klanten", for that source, resting on the claim and the mark. Clearing the mark or undoing the decision withdraws it, and the source dropping the person resolves it. A list kept by hand raises none. See Change requests.

    On the page: a contradiction about a person is decided on that person's row in People (the warning icon's popover: both statements, View in transcript on a claim for an analyst, and the choices); one about the unit itself (a property against the value it holds) sits in a Contradictions (n) panel inside What we know. The analyst's attention line under the header counts them all ("2 to decide") and jumps to them: People filtered to To decide, or What we know opened. A reader sees the open ones without choices.

    The review list and What we know¶

    • What we know holds facts about the unit only — its description, the properties the sources established, its other names, the same unit as other kinds of member, and More details — folded by default to one line: the start of the description, then "3 properties · also known as HA · 2 to review" (the review count only for an analyst, and only the unit's own claims; a person's are on their row).
    • To review: the attention line's "13 to review" opens one drawer with every proposed claim — about the unit and its people — in the review list; the same claim can also be decided where it shows (a person's row, What we know). The list shows the high tier first; the low tier waits folded under Less relevant (n). Reject all less relevant and Reject all descriptions each send one request.

    The transcript view¶

    View (per source in the Sources list) opens a side panel, Source: the text as a dialogue — a speaker column ("Name:" at the start of a line; a .vtt / .srt transcript arrives as such lines) beside their words — with every claim's quote highlighted. A colour per kind (role, role history, membership, alias, property, description); the fill for the status: a light fill with a dashed underline while proposed, a solid fill once confirmed, grey and struck through when rejected; a legend says which is which. A highlight is a keyboard-focusable button with an aria label naming its claims; it opens the claim card (kind, person, value, period, status, Confirm / Reject while proposed). In transcript next to a claim in the review list — and a claim icon on a person's row in People — opens the panel scrolled to the quote, which pulses. Escape closes it.

    The spans come from the API: stored at extraction (and for an analyst's claim), found from the verbatim quote when the transcript is opened for a claim from before 100. A quote may run over a line break into the next turn (highlighted in both, never in the speaker column) and quotes may overlap (the piece lists every claim, takes the shorter quote's style and an outline). The view cuts each turn only at highlight boundaries, in one pass over the turns, and renders 2,000 turns at a time (Show more), always as far as the focused quote — a transcript at the 5,000,000-character cap is not rendered in one go.

    On the Timeline¶

    The context's Timeline (GET /contexts/:id/timeline, routes/recentChanges/timeline.js) merges the context row's own _history with what its interviews did (contexts/interviews/timelineEvents.js, two statements in store.js, eventKind: 'interview', a violet dot): "Interview with X uploaded (file)", "n claims extracted from the interview with X", each decision — "Confirmed: Membership — Jan Smit (former member)", "Rejected: …" — and a confirmed claim that changed something reads as that change: "Piet set as Responsible — from the interview with X, date" (linked to the holder), "Also known as: Havenkantoor added — …", "sector set to logistics — …", "Description extended — …". Decisions are read from decidedAt and the role a claim set from ContextRoles.sourceClaimId. A reader gets confirmed decisions only — no uploads, no extraction counts, nothing rejected.

    The context's _history part now leaves out updates that only moved bookkeeping (lastCalculatedAt, updatedAt, the member counts) in SQL, before the LIMIT: the plugin runner re-stamps lastCalculatedAt every few minutes, which had filled the whole page of 50 rows; the page hid those, and the Timeline came up empty.

    Who sees what¶

    • Analyst (data.write.contexts): everything — Add information, the attention line, the review, the sources, the suggestions.
    • Business reader (no data.write.contexts) and context owner (the read-only shared page; …/claims and …/people are on the owner allow-list): confirmed claims only — the API narrows the lists, the page does not filter them. No Add information, no review buttons, no sources, no transcripts. The People overview, Former included, is theirs too: who left and what they still hold is read from confirmed claims and decisions.
    • The transcript stays analyst-only. Showing it to a reader would show the words behind proposed and rejected claims too, and deciding which parts a reader may read is not cheap; the view is offered only with data.write.contexts (the transcript GET already required it).

    On the page¶

    • The page structure is in Template UI: People → What we know → Sources → Change requests → Sub-units → Timeline.
    • People overview: each person once, with their status, role, sources, access and the claims as icons (above).
    • What we know (folded to one line): the unit's own facts, with each source and the quote on expand; for an analyst also its own claims to review.
    • Who answers for it (under the name in the header): interview suggestions under Accountable and Responsible, next to the best guess, with Confirm / Not this person / Choose someone else.
    • Account and person pages: Mentioned in interviews, linking each context.

    Known limits¶

    • A synced context's re-sync rewrites what its source sends: an upload replaces extendedAttributes, so a property or an alias confirmed from an interview is overwritten by the next re-import of that list. The confirmed claim stays under What we know.
    • A person in a membership claim is matched once, at extraction; an account of that person created later is not linked to the old claim.
    • Extraction runs inside the API process; a restart during a run leaves it running, and Read again is allowed once it is 30 minutes old. The status says Waiting for the model… before a run starts and Reading… while it goes; from two minutes on it says how long, and that the model may be busy with another request — the API cannot tell waiting from reading, so it does not pretend to.
    • A decided claim cannot be undecided; reverse its effect by hand (edit the description, remove the role).
    • Not in this slice: audio, speaker diarisation, merging claims across interviews, contradictions between interviews, claims about resources.
