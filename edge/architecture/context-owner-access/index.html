    Context-owner access¶

    A department's manager or an application's owner usually has no Identity Atlas role, yet they are exactly the person an access review is held with. Context-owner access lets them sign in with their normal Microsoft account and land on their own context — nothing else. It is the same idea as sharing a matrix, without a link: the owner simply opens Identity Atlas.

    Relative permissions¶

    Context-owner access is one of two relative permissions: access somebody gets because of how they relate to the data, rather than because they hold an Identity Atlas role. Both are read-only and limited to the data the person relates to:

    Kind Relation to the data Decided by
    Context owner Accountable or Responsible for a context The admin switch below, then the ContextRoles rows
    Matrix-share recipient A saved matrix was shared with them Whoever shares the matrix, per share (Sharing a matrix)

    The Roles & Permissions admin tab groups them in a Relative permissions section below the role table. Matrix sharing has no setting there and its behaviour is unchanged. Today the two are enforced separately: a context owner by the allow-list and the tiers on this page (the MCP server reuses both — The MCP server), a share recipient by the share itself. Running matrix shares under the same data-level scope is a later, separate track (#1105).

    Turning it on¶

    Admin → Roles & Permissions → Relative permissions → Context owners (needs admin.auth). Off by default. The setting is stored in WorkerConfig as CONTEXT_OWNER_ACCESS and takes effect on the next request; no restart.

    Below it, Context owners may also use the MCP server (CONTEXT_OWNER_MCP, off by default) lets the same people ask about their contexts from an AI assistant, under exactly the same scope — see The MCP server. It only has effect while the first switch is on.

    API: GET / PUT /api/admin/context-owner-access with { "enabled": true } and/or { "mcp": true }; a field left out keeps its value.

    Who qualifies¶

    A signed-in user qualifies when all of these hold:

    • authentication is enabled and the switch is on;
    • they would otherwise have no access: their Entra app roles map to no permission, or they lack a role listed in AUTH_REQUIRED_ROLES;
    • they are matched to our data (auth/resolveMe.js: token oid = account id, else a unique email match), and a ContextRoles row (Accountable or Responsible) names their account, their person (Identity), or another account linked to that person.

    Somebody who holds any real permission is not affected: they keep the ordinary app, unscoped. Somebody who qualifies on everything except holding a context role is refused, as anyone without a role is: the "no role" 403, or the AUTH_REQUIRED_ROLES 403. Owner mode is the only way in without a role.

    Their scope¶

    The contexts an owner may open are:

    1. the contexts they hold a role on — Accountable and Responsible count the same;
    2. all the sub-units below those (descendants through parentContextId, to any depth) — also a sub-unit that has an Accountable or Responsible of its own: accountability is hierarchical, whoever answers for a unit answers for everything below it. That sub-unit's owner, in turn, gets only their own subtree — nothing walks upward;
    3. the variants of the contexts they hold (SameUnitAs — the same unit as people, accounts or resources). A sub-unit's variants are not added.
    Example tree Ann is Accountable for Finance In Ann's scope
    Finance (Ann) yes — held
    ├ Accounts payable no roles yes — sub-unit
    │ └ AP team North no roles yes — sub-unit
    └ Treasury Bob is Responsible yes — sub-unit (Bob's too)
      └ Treasury ops no roles yes — sub-unit

    Bob, Responsible for Treasury, gets Treasury and Treasury ops — not Finance, not Accounts payable.

    The scope is computed on every request (auth/contextOwner.js findOwnedSubUnits, one recursive statement over the indexed parentContextId with a cycle guard — a top-level owner with thousands of units costs one round trip), so a change in the tree takes effect immediately.

    What they can see¶

    Request (GET only) Allowed when
    /api/auth-me, /api/features, /api/version, /api/preferences, /api/attribute-labels always (the app shell)
    /api/contexts/:id, …/members, …/roles, …/relationships, …/claims (confirmed claims only), …/timeline :id is in scope
    /api/contexts/:id/members/:memberId/references :id is in scope and :memberId is a member of :id itself (checked against ContextMembers; an id from another context, or from one of this context's sub-units, is refused)
    /api/risk-scores/contexts/:id :id is in scope
    /api/reports/application-access-review/export contexts lists only in-scope ids, and every context those values resolve to is in scope; the applications alias and names are refused
    /api/reports/unit-members/export the same contexts check; with includeSubUnits (any value) also every descendant of what the contexts resolve to must be in scope — true below a held context, not below a variant

    | One object's detail reads — /api/user/:id (and its memberships, contexts, activity, timeline, diff, …), /api/identities/:id (…), /api/resources/:id, /api/group/:id, /api/access-package/:id (…), /api/org-chart/user/:id/manager|reports, /api/identities/by-user/:id, /api/risk-scores/{users,identities,groups,resources,business-roles}/:id | the object opens in full for this owner (see Exploring the team); the list is auth/ownerAccess/endpoints.js | | /api/access-package-groups — the business-role catalogue the matrix maps roles to their groups with | always, but the response is cut to the business roles the owner may open (auth/ownerAccess/responses.js): those their team holds, or that are themselves in scope — never the whole catalogue | | POST /api/matrix/data — the one POST, a read | the filter is bound to the owner's team (see The team matrix) |

    Everything else — any other path, and every non-GET (but the team matrix) — answers 403 { "error": "Context owners can only open their own context" }.

    A member's references — names only¶

    For each member of a context in scope, the owner can see what that member is directly tied to:

    Member Groups shown
    An account or a person Groups & resources they hold (with the assignment type: Direct, Indirect or Eligible), Business roles they hold, Other contexts they are in. A person's are read through the person and every linked account, once per resource and assignment type
    A resource Held by — accounts by name and type, or a person where the assignment was made to the person; Business roles that contain it; Other contexts it is in
    A system Other contexts it is in

    The answer is a reference projection (contexts/memberReferences.js): every item is a display name plus the few words needed to read it — resource type, system name, assignment type, holder type, context type. It carries no ids. There is nothing in it to follow, and the detail endpoints of the referenced objects stay off the allow-list: an owner sees that a member holds "Finance-Admins" there. To open "Finance-Admins" itself they go through the member's own page (Exploring the team), where every reference carries the API's access flag.

    Each group lists at most 50 names, alphabetically, with the true total (the page says "+ 1,234 more"). Everything is shown; nothing is held back by sensitivity today. Leaving out references by tag (for example a "Sensitive" tag context) is a possible later step.

    The endpoint is open to every ordinary reader as well; only the owner's page shows it, as an expandable member row.

    The owner's page¶

    In the UI an owner gets a bare page (no main navigation): their context directly, or a list on the left when they hold several. The page is the ordinary context page in its business (reading) view, read-only: no Edit, no role pencils, no suggestion, no member editing, no Copy link or Open as page. People, members and role holders link to their page when the API marks them accessible (components/EntityLink.jsx); an opened page or the team matrix shows on top of the unit, with Back (components/contextOwner/).

    • Sub-units open in place — all of them, whoever else owns them. The breadcrumb names the direct parent without reading it, and opens it in place when it is in scope (a sub-unit's way back up).
    • A variant opens in place only when it is in scope.
    • Each member expands to its references, grouped and as plain text: on a unit of people or accounts the name of each person in the People overview whom the member list holds (not a former member, nor someone only an interview names); elsewhere each row of the members list, but not while Include sub-units is on. The API answers only for a context's own members. Beside the name, Open opens the member's full page.
    • The Actions area drops View in matrix, which the owner cannot open, and the "Coming soon" survey and review cards, which are the analyst's to start; it keeps the report downloads, which run on the context itself: Download entitlement report on an application and Download unit report on a unit of accounts or people, next to Team matrix; a context of resources offers Application matrix.

    contexts/ContextOwnerScopeContext.jsx carries the scope into the page; components/contexts/contextOpener.js decides which sub-units and parents are links.

    Exploring the team¶

    From their unit an owner can open the people in it and what those people hold; from an application, the people who hold its resources — the full detail page with every tab (Attributes, Relationships, Timeline, Diff, and Risk read-only) — and no further. Whether an object opens is decided by the API from the data, in four tiers (auth/ownerAccess/tiers.js):

    Tier What Opens
    team a member of an in-scope context. Accounts and people cross over both ways: an account whose person is a member, a person one of whose accounts is a member. A resource that is itself a member (a unit of resources, an application) in full
    held a resource a team member holds: a live Direct, Indirect or Eligible assignment made to a team account or a team person in full
    holder the mirror for a context of resources (an application): an account or person holding — live Direct, Indirect or Eligible — a resource that is a member of an in-scope context. Same crossover as the team: an account whose person holds one, a person one of whose accounts does in full
    — everything else name only

    One step out, no further: what a holder holds outside the application is not held (that is the team's only), so it is name-only. An owner of several contexts — say a unit and an application — gets the union of the tiers over all of them; an object that is both team and holder is reported as team.

    A detail read about an object that is in none of these tiers answers the same 403 as any other refused path. Facts are never stripped: a group's page lists all its members, people outside the unit included. Instead, in owner mode only, every referenced object in the response carries accessible: true | false (auth/ownerAccess/annotate.js, driven by the per-path table in auth/ownerAccess/endpoints.js; a second reference on one row writes its own flag, such as principalAccessible). The UI shows an accessible object as a link and any other as lighter plain text with the hint "No access to details" (components/EntityLink.jsx); it never decides on its own. An ordinary caller's responses are unchanged and no classification runs for them.

    Writes stay refused: the risk override, a linked-account override and every other non-GET answer 403.

    Scale. The tiers are checked per object, in one batch per kind for a response: team through the ContextMembers primary key and the IdentityMembers indexes, held as an EXISTS through ix_RA_resourceId, holder as an EXISTS through ix_RA_principalId / the identityId index, each assignment checked against the ContextMembers primary key — never a load of the team's or the application's assignments. Answers are cached for the request and, across requests, for 60 seconds per (owner scope, kind, id); the scope itself is still resolved on every request.

    The team and application matrices¶

    Each context page in the owner's scope offers its own matrix, opened inside the owner's page: Team matrix on a unit of accounts or people (its members as rows, what they hold as columns) and Application matrix on a context of resources (its resources as columns, everyone holding one as a row — the holders). POST /api/matrix/data runs for an owner only when (auth/ownerAccess/matrixFilter.js):

    • every context condition anywhere in the filter names an in-scope context; with includeChildren, every descendant of it must be in scope too (always true below a held context; a variant does not bring its sub-units along);
    • an axis is anchored to one of them: the subject include on a unit of accounts or people, or the resource include on a context of resources (the include conditions of an axis are ANDed, so the anchor bounds it; the flat grid lists only subjects with a cell in the columns, so a column bound also bounds the rows to holders). A context of the wrong kind on an axis is dropped by that axis's query and is never an anchor;
    • the org-unit roll-up (sortHierarchy, or rollupKind: 'context' with rollupContextId / rollupPath / rollupExpanded) is kept when its root is an in-scope context whose whole subtree is in scope and every node it names is in scope — a root or node elsewhere is refused; its cells and member counts run through the same subject filter, so the anchor still bounds them;
    • the other aggregate modes (attribute roll-up and fold, roles-only drill, inherited access) are removed.

    Each is exactly the matrix an analyst opens from that context ("View in matrix", utils/matrixFilter.js unitMatrixFilter), rendered by the ordinary matrix views in their read-only mode — the owner never gets a reduced grid. On a unit it opens grouped by its sub-units — the unit's own tree as collapsible column groups, as an analyst's hierarchy sort, expanded the same way, with the unit's own people as a "direct members" column beside its sub-units (a unit without sub-units is just that column) and the business-role columns; Group by unit off gives the flat standard matrix, with the business-role band, governed cells, the groups a business role contains coloured by it, and the All / Governed / Non-governed / Gaps lenses; those come from the matrix data (managedByPackages) and the business-role catalogue, which is answered for an owner cut to the roles they may open. Anything else is a 403. The matrix's other reads (tags, column value lists, the default filter, nested-group expansion, the inheritance path) are not on the list; the views carry on without them (no tag chips, no expand arrow on a nested group). The application matrix has no org roll-up: the roll-up groups people by their unit tree, and an application's tree is one of resources.

    Reusing this scope (API code)¶

    Everything another surface needs to answer on an owner's behalf is exported from app/api/src/auth/ownerAccess/index.js:

    import { createOwnerAccess, ownerMatrixVerdict, annotateReferences, OPENABLE_SQL } from '../auth/ownerAccess/index.js';

const owner = await resolveContextOwner(user);              // auth/contextOwner.js — null for a non-owner
const access = createOwnerAccess(owner.contextIds);         // one per request
await access.canOpen('user', id);                          // may this object be opened in full?
await access.tierOf('resource', id);                       // 'team' | 'held' | null (people: 'team' | 'holder' | null)
await access.accessibleIds('principal', ids);              // batch: the Set of ids that open
ownerMatrixVerdict(body, owner.contextIds, contextTypes);   // { ok, filter } for a matrix query
await annotateReferences(body, refs, access);               // mark references in a result
OPENABLE_SQL.resource('t0."id"', '$1::uuid[]');             // the same rule as an SQL predicate, for inside a query

    Kinds accept the data model's words (principal, identity, resource, context, member) and the UI's (user, group, access-package, business-role). OPENABLE_SQL is what the tier statements themselves are built from, so a query that embeds it agrees with canOpen by construction.

    The MCP server¶

    With Context owners may also use the MCP server on, an owner can connect Claude, ChatGPT or Copilot (MCP server) and gets exactly the scope on this page: every query the assistant runs is compiled with OPENABLE_SQL on its own table, inside every relation and inside every relation column; name lookups and search are restricted the same way; a report runs only when this page's allow-list would let the owner download it. Every answer says it is restricted. Design, enforcement points and the regression suite that keeps REST and MCP in step: MCP and relative permissions.

    Enforcement¶

    Enforced by the API, not the UI:

    • middleware/auth.js hands a validated caller with no access to middleware/contextOwnerMode.js, which decides once per request (the middleware runs at every router mount) and fails closed with a 500 if a lookup errors.
    • auth/contextOwnerScope.js is the allow-list — a table, so a route added to the API later is closed to owners until somebody lists it on purpose. Where the answer needs the database (a report's named contexts, a member's membership) the verdict says what to check and the middleware checks it.
    • auth/contextOwner.js holds the lookups (roles, sub-units, variants, and the report's name-or-id resolution), proven against PostgreSQL by contract-tests/contextOwner.contract.test.js; the reference projection is proven by contract-tests/memberReferences.contract.test.js.

    With authentication off¶

    Nothing changes: in open mode everybody already sees everything, and nobody is treated as a context owner.

    Known limits¶

    • A context owner who is also sent a matrix share link lands on their context page instead: the shared matrix reads endpoints outside an owner's scope.
    • Whether someone is an owner, and of which sub-units, is read on every request, so adding or removing a role takes effect immediately; there is no cache to expire.
    • A member's references (the expandable row) are direct only (one hop), names only, and capped at 50 per group; the full lists are on the member's own page.
    • The team and application matrices open on one context with all its sub-contexts (an application often keeps its resources in child contexts); a variant's matrix is refused when the variant has sub-contexts outside the scope.
    • A variant's own sub-units are not in scope, so a report or matrix of a variant with its sub-units is refused.
