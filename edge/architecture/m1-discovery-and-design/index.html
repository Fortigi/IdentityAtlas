    Authorization workbench — Milestone 1 design¶

    Status: proof of concept (draft PR, stacked on the context-readability PR). Built from the Milestone 1 brief. This page records what was reused, what was added, the decisions taken without a review round, and what is left open.

    What it does¶

    An analyst opens an organizational unit, clicks Authorization workbench, and gets a full-width tab (#workbench:<unit id>) with:

    • the unit's people (one row per person, every linked account folded in; an account nobody linked is its own row) and what they hold (the matrix's own data);
    • what is known of their work — the activities they perform, their expertise and the duties they hold beside it — as chips with their status (✓ confirmed, ? proposed, ! disputed) and, on click, the evidence: source, quote, who decided;
    • one row per person, with Excel-like columns on the left (name, job title, chosen system data, activity, expertise, duty): filter any column by its values (a multi-value column keeps a row when any value is ticked; proposed values can be left out; filters combine with AND), sort by a header, and take the people shown into the candidate in one click; grouping is optional;
    • select cells (click, Shift-click for a rectangle, Ctrl/⌘-click to add; a person, a group or a column header selects what they hold) and mark them: other context, unrelated here, needs investigation;
    • a candidate role — new, or a proposed update to a business role the unit already holds — with members and entitlements chosen separately, a deterministic first proposal (columns at least 50 % / 75 % / all of the members hold, minus what was marked other context or unrelated), and
    • the candidate shown as Draft in the matrix's business-role band, every cell the matrix's own (MatrixCell): covered = the role's colour, potential gain = its provisioning gap, a candidate entitlement a non-member holds = held outside; plain = outside the candidate. Never "loss".

    Nothing in the workbench changes access or is saved. The candidate lives in the browser tab.

    The global matrix is not touched.

    Schema decisions¶

    1. Relations are claims. A link "Bram holds Works council member" is a ContextClaims row of kind duty, subject the person (identity or account), value the name, objectContextId the object. One store for everything a source says, one review flow, one history. A claim never creates access and never changes ContextMembers.
    2. Objects are contexts. Activity, Expertise, Duty contexts (manual, template Tag, targetType Identity) under roots marked extendedAttributes.knowledgeRoot. They are browsable and renamable on the Contexts page. A proposed claim may not name an object yet; confirming it finds one by name (case and spacing aside, or an alias) or creates it.
    3. "Duty", not "responsibility". The brief's holdsResponsibility is called duty in code and on screen, so it is never read as the ContextRole Responsible (who does the day-to-day for a unit or an application). The ontology keeps the brief's predicate names in each kind's description (performsActivity, hasExpertise, holdsResponsibility).
    4. Link state is read from all claims about one (person, kind, name): confirmed (someone confirmed it, nobody rejected it), disputed (confirmed or proposed by one source, rejected for another), proposed (only proposed), rejected (only rejected — not shown in groups).
    5. Multi-value columns. One row per person; a person with two activities has two chips, and a filter keeps the row when any value is ticked. People with nothing known show as (Not known) in the filter — missing is never negative evidence. Optional grouping shows a person under each value.
    6. Scope. The unit and, by default, its sub-units (toggle). At most 400 people and 300 columns (most held first; a unit with more than 120 columns opens on columns at least two people hold).
    7. Read cost. Eleven statements whatever the unit's size (load.test.js pins the list); about 25 ms for a 9-person unit with 500 held resources on the demo box.

    How a proposed role is assigned¶

    Every new proposed role first asks its kind (two cards; changeable later):

    Kind Members Rule means Joiners Leavers Assignment origin
    Rule-based — assigned automatically computed from the rule (+ always include − always exclude); without a rule, chosen by hand who holds it add automatically / ask for approval remove automatically / ask for review / keep Automatic
    Requestable — people request it and someone approves chosen by hand: the expected initial holders who may request it (optional) — (a request) on leaving the unit: remove / review / keep Requested
    • The rule is the workbench's filters. Scope = the unit, with or without its sub-units; conditions = the column filters (AND across columns, ANY ticked value or "contains" text within one; none = everyone in scope). "Use these filters as the role's rule" copies them. The rule shows as one sentence ("Everyone in Property Claims and its sub-units whose job title contains “handler” or is Team lead Property — added automatically, removed when they no longer match"), with "matches now: N" and "would also apply to people who join the unit later".
    • Approver (requestable): the unit's Accountable, the role's owner, or a named person (the context role person picker).
    • Rationale: a free-text "why this role".
    • Implementability: a condition on activity, expertise or duty rests on Identity Atlas knowledge the IGA system does not hold — noted ("can't be enforced automatically there"), not blocked. Job title, department and unit conditions are source data.
    • Vocabulary. This is the role's assignment rule, the counterpart of the governance model's AssignmentPolicies (Entra access-package auto-assignment policy → rule-based; a request policy → requestable; Omada assignment policy). A Propose update of an existing role prefills the kind from its crawled policies (policyKind: an auto-add rule → rule-based, a policy without one → requestable), else asks. Proposals never write to AssignmentPolicies (crawled data).
    • Nothing is enforced anywhere; the impact overlay shows the active role's computed members.
    • Saved with the investigation (migration 108): RoleProposals.assignmentKind, membershipRule (jsonb), joinerPolicy, leaverPolicy, approver (jsonb), rationale; "always exclude" people as RoleProposalMembers with kind = 'exclude'.

    Impact semantics¶

    Observed In candidate (member) Shown as
    yes yes Covered — the candidate would explain it
    no yes Potential gain — the candidate would add it
    yes no Outside the candidate — not a removal; other grants may keep it
    yes marked Other context / Unrelated here / Needs investigation
    yes — (not a member) held, unchanged

    Removing access is not modelled in M1: the panel says so ("impact unknown") instead of predicting a loss. When a candidate updates an existing role, the panel lists what it adds to and leaves out of the role's composition; holders may keep what is left out through other grants.

    Demo data¶

    tools/demo-workbench/workbench-fixture.sql loads a fictional insurer ("Northwind Insurance (synthetic)"): a Claims Handling unit of 26 people in two teams, two systems, an existing business role held by 8 of 12 motor handlers, a person with two accounts, a manager interview whose claims are confirmed, proposed and disputed, and two works-council members whose works-council access must stay out of a claims role. Idempotent; it touches nothing else.

    Open questions for review¶

    1. Should a confirmed link also add the person as a member of the object context, so the global matrix can filter by activity through the existing context filters? (Not done: two mechanisms for one fact.)
    2. Is duty the right word in Dutch and English UI ("nevenfunctie", "extra taak")?
    3. Should job title / seniority be a fourth dimension in the ontology, or stay a source attribute?
    4. Who may see the workbench: analysts only (now data.write.contexts), or also a unit's Accountable?

    M2a — save and reopen¶

    The first slice of Milestone 2: an analyst saves the workbench as an investigation on the unit and opens it again later. No review, approval or versions yet (M2b), but the tables are shaped for them.

    What is saved (migration 107):

    Table Holds
    WorkbenchInvestigations One saved session on a unit: name, description, the view (people columns, filters, sort, sub-units in or out, column threshold), when the access data on screen was read, who saved it and when. Unique name per unit (case and spaces ignored). Org-wide, like saved matrices.
    WorkbenchAccessSnapshots The held cells on screen at the last save (person\|resource\|type), kept apart so the history trigger does not copy them on every save.
    WorkbenchMarks Person × resource × mark (other / unrelated / investigate), an optional note, who marked it and when.
    RoleProposals Each proposed role: name, description, the business role it updates (if any), its place in the list, status (draft only for now) and version (1 for now).
    RoleProposalMembers / RoleProposalEntitlements / RoleProposalEvidence The people it is for, what it would contain, and the claims it rests on.

    Decisions

    • A proposed role is never a Resources row. As a business role it would show up in the matrix, reports and counts as if it existed. Updating an existing role only points at it (baseBusinessRoleId, set to null if that role disappears).
    • People and resources carry no foreign key. A full sync can delete an identity (that is how the demo unit once went empty); a saved investigation must then keep its rows and say "no longer found", not silently lose them. A person is the workbench's own row key: an identity, or an account linked to no identity.
    • A save holds the whole draft. A mark or proposed role the draft no longer holds is removed; one proposed role can also be deleted on its own (DELETE …/proposals/:id). An unchanged mark keeps who made it; an unchanged proposed role keeps its updatedAt; a proposed role keeps its id.
    • No silent overwrite. A save sends the updatedAt it opened; when someone saved since, it is refused (409) with who did. The analyst can save under another name.
    • Reopening never blocks on changed data. The workbench compares the saved snapshot with the access data on screen and says how many cells differ ("held then and not now", "held now and not then", "held another way"); marks and proposed roles stay as saved.

    API (needs data.write.contexts, like the workbench): GET|POST /api/contexts/:id/workbench/investigations, GET|PUT|DELETE /api/workbench/investigations/:id, DELETE /api/workbench/investigations/:id/proposals/:proposalId.

    UI: a strip under the workbench header shows the state ("Nothing to save yet", "Not saved", "Saved as …", "Unsaved changes to …") with Save, Save as… and Open… (the unit's saved investigations with who saved them, when, and when their access data was read; opening one over unsaved work and deleting one ask first). The proposed-roles badge says not saved, saved or unsaved changes.

    Proposed roles in the matrix¶

    Next to the lens switch (All / Governed / Non-governed / Gaps) the Matrix has a toggle, + Proposed roles. It is not a lens: it adds the saved proposed roles on top of whichever lens is chosen, and that lens shows exactly what it shows without the toggle (decided 2026-10-29).

    • Who sees it: someone with data.write.contexts, outside a shared view, while organisation knowledge is on.
    • Off: the Matrix is exactly as before.
    • Session only: the toggle is session state in app/MatrixArea. It is not part of the matrix definition, so a saved or shared matrix never carries it.

    With it on (matrix/ProposedMatrix):

    • Each saved proposed role is a band "Draft: " under the category Proposed, in its own colour. The cells it covers take that colour (the matrix's own describeCell / MatrixCell). A member who lacks one of its entitlements shows as the matrix's provisioning gap.
    • The lens filters stay real. Governed and Non-governed still decide from the real business-role coverage (managedByPackages). The proposals' cells reach only the colouring (extraCoverage, merged into the colour map by buildManagedApMap).
    • In Non-governed, where the business-role bands are hidden, the Draft bands still show (bandsForLens). In Gaps, a proposed role's provisioning gaps show like a business role's.
    • The Draft columns come last, after every real business role (categorised or not), and the real ones keep their order (compareAccessPackages). Where the drafts start there is a gap with a faint dashed line, and "Proposed" in the band above them (isProposedStart, PROPOSED_GAP_CLASS), in the band, names row and body cells alike so the columns line up.
    • The real business roles keep their bands and colours, so an overlap with what is already governed shows.
    • The rows are the view's own; the toggle reads no rows. Members of a proposed role who are not in the view are not added.
    • Who it reaches is computed now. A rule-based role's saved rule runs on the unit's current people (the workbench's own effectiveMembers), plus always-include minus always-exclude. A requestable role uses its saved members.
    • From investigation picks one saved investigation (unit, saved by, date) or all of them.

    API (needs data.write.contexts): GET /api/workbench/investigations lists every saved investigation holding a proposed role, any unit. Read-only. The toggle adds to the view's own rows, so it needs no rows of its own. A "members outside this view" option would need an endpoint for those accounts' rows; it is not built.

    People as columns (the matrix's orientation)¶

    With people as rows, someone holding 100+ groups means an endless horizontal scrollbar. That is why the global matrix puts resources on the rows and people on the columns. The workbench now opens that way too. People as rows stays one click away, and the choice is saved with the investigation's view as orientation.

    Only the layout changes (workbench.transposed.js, WorkbenchColumnsGrid). The cells are still person × resource, so the following work the same in both orientations: selection (click, Shift-click rectangle, Ctrl/⌘-click), marks, the candidate, rules, impact and save.

    • Rows: each business role of the band — the unit's roles, then the proposed ones as "Draft: …" — in its matrix colour, with its entitlements nested under it, then "Other access".
    • A role row folds.
    • A role lists every entitlement of it the unit knows. Even a rarely held one shows, so the threshold and Find access narrow only "Other access".
    • A resource two roles contain shows under each.
    • Columns: the people shown (filtered, sorted).
    • The chosen people columns become header rows above them, the display name last, next to the cells as in the matrix.
    • Each header row's label has the same sort and Excel-like filter.
    • A narrow cell shows the first value written upwards: knowledge with ✓/?/!, then "+N". Hovering lists all values; a click opens their evidence, with Confirm / Reject.
    • A header row nobody has a value in stays one line high.
    • Grouping (Group by) is a people-as-rows feature, so it is hidden with people as columns.
    • The rows are virtualised with TanStack Virtual, as the matrix's are.

    The grid reuses the matrix's MatrixCell and colours. It does not reuse MatrixGroupRow or SortableMatrixBody. Those carry the global matrix's own state (nested-group expansion, drag order, fold tallies), and threading the workbench's selection and marks through them would put the global matrix at risk.

    One scrollbar: the page's¶

    The page scrolls; the workbench grid has no vertical scrollbar of its own (decided with Wim 2026-10-29, after a first version sized the grid to the window and left room for one row under the header rows).

    • Header: the grid's header rows stick to the top of the window as the page scrolls (PageStickyTable).
    • Wide grids: a grid wider than its column scrolls sideways inside itself. A box that scrolls is what a sticky header sticks to, so the header lives in a strip of its own above the body.
    • The strip uses the same fixed column widths, clipped sideways (overflow-x: clip, so the filter menus and popovers that open from it are not cut off).
    • It moves with the body's horizontal scroll; its row labels move back, so they stay put.
    • With people as columns, the five permission columns stay in place too.
    • Virtualisation: rows are virtualised against the page (usePageVirtualRows, TanStack's window virtualiser).
    • The candidate panel sticks to the top of the window beside the grid, and scrolls within itself only when it is taller than the window.
    • Header rows about people are as tall as their longest value needs, within tight limits (headerRowHeight: values up to 44px — the ✓/? mark and the first few characters — names up to 88px); the whole value shows on hover. A row nobody has a value in is one line.
    • Measured (Fortigi Members, people as columns): 26 permission rows in view without scrolling at 2254×1351, 16 at 1920×1080, 4 at 1366×768. Once scrolled the header sits at the top of the window and 20+ rows show.

    Filtering the permissions¶

    The permissions shown are filtered the way the people are (workbench.permissions.js): an Excel-like filter and sort on each of the matrix's row cells — Resource name, Contexts, Type, System, Held by — AND across columns.

    • Contexts are the tags and logical applications a resource belongs to (ContextMembers).
    • The workbench API returns them per column (columns[].contexts, the matrix's own join, matrix/resourceContexts.js), plus those contexts with every parent (contexts, recursive on parentContextId).
    • A context takes its sub-contexts: choosing a logical application also takes the resources in its modules.
    • The filter goes both ways: Only these, or Hide resources in these (e.g. everything tagged "Default access").
    • Held by: "Access held by at least N" stays, plus at most (outliers), with Hide permissions only one person holds as the at-least-2 preset.
    • Business roles:
    • An entitlement the filters hide is not under its role either. A role whose entitlements are all hidden says "(all hidden by filters)".
    • A proposed role keeps all of its own, the hidden ones last and marked "hidden by filter". Nothing is silently dropped from a proposal.
    • Impact counts only what is shown, and says "N permissions hidden by filters — not counted".
    • The filters, the permission sort and the held-by range are part of the saved view (permissionFilters, permissionSort, maxHolders).
    • With people as rows the permissions are columns, and the same filters sit behind a Permissions menu in the toolbar.

    Proposed roles on the unit page¶

    An analyst's unit page shows the same saved proposals. It reads them from the existing investigation endpoints, so no new API.

    • The Authorization workbench button carries "N proposed".
    • A Proposed roles fold ("3 in 2 investigations") lists each saved proposed role with:
    • its colour (the workbench's band index: the unit's existing roles first);
    • its name and kind;
    • its rule as one sentence, or "chosen by hand";
    • the people it reaches now and its entitlements;
    • the investigation, who saved it and when.

    The rows are read only when the fold is opened. Only this unit's investigations are listed, not a sub-unit's. - A row opens the workbench on #workbench:<unit>:<investigation>:<proposed role> (workbench.link.js). The workbench opens that investigation once and puts that role in the panel.

    What we know agrees with the workbench. The workbench finds activity, expertise and duty claims by person. The unit page used to read only the claims filed on the unit. An interview held on the parent unit names what the people of a sub-unit do, so the sub-unit's page said "Nothing recorded yet". GET /api/contexts/:id/claims now also returns the knowledge claims about the unit's members, wherever filed: - the members' identities and accounts, each widened by the identity ↔ account links; - members removed by an override are left out; - each such claim names where it was filed (filedUnderName).

    What we know counts and lists them, and a proposed one is reviewable there. The People and Timeline loaders are unchanged.

    Deferred (Milestone 2 / 3)¶

    • M2b: review, approve and version a proposed role (status beyond draft, version beyond 1, a compare between versions), export it, and optionally raise a change request to the IGA system once approved.
    • M3: compare an approved proposal with a later snapshot (implemented as proposed? drift?).
    • A computed first proposal of entitlements (e.g. what at least half of the members hold): tried and taken out — the analyst selects entitlements. Revisit with real analyst feedback.
    • Removal simulation with grant-path proof; keyboard range selection; virtualised grid for very large units.
