    Reading a detail page¶

    Before this page

    Assumes you have read Reading the matrix. Brand new? Start at The words you need first.

    The matrix tells you that an account holds a resource. A detail page tells you everything else about one thing: who this account is, who is in this group, what this business role grants, and what changed last month. Accounts, identities, resources and business roles each have a detail page, and all four are built the same way, so learning one teaches you the rest.

    Opening a detail page¶

    A detail page opens as its own tab in the top navigation, next to Matrix, Resources and the others. You can keep several open and switch between them. Close one with the × on its tab or in the page's top-right corner.

    From the matrix:

    Click Opens
    A resource's name at the start of a row The resource
    An account's name in a column header The account
    An identity's name in a column header (in a matrix of identities) The identity
    A business role's name in the header of its coloured column The business role
    A BR chip next to a resource name The other business role that grants that resource

    From the lists: click a name on the Principals (Users), Resources, Business Roles or Identities tab.

    From another detail page: every name that appears as a link (an owner, a member, a group, a linked account) opens that item's own detail page.

    What every detail page has¶

    The header¶

    The top of the page says what you are looking at: the name, a badge for its kind, and the few facts you most often need. What those facts are differs per kind and is described further down. An item that no longer exists in the system it came from carries a Deleted in source badge.

    The tabs¶

    Tab The question it answers
    Attributes What do we know about this item?
    Relationships What is it connected to: who holds it, what does it hold, who owns it?
    Timeline What changed, and when?
    Diff What is different now compared with a date in the past?
    Risk How risky is it, and why?

    The Risk tab is only there when risk scoring is switched on and the item has a score.

    Attributes¶

    One table with every attribute Identity Atlas holds for the item, as name | value. The number on the tab is how many attributes there are. Attributes that are specific to the source system carry a small ext tag; they are just as real as the others, the tag only tells you they are not part of the common set every system has.

    Facts already shown in the header (such as the name and the description) are not repeated in the table.

    Relationships¶

    A diagram with the item in the middle and one circle around it for every kind of connection. The number in a circle is how many there are; a circle with 0 is greyed out.

    • Click a circle to fan it out into the individual items. The same items appear as a list under the diagram, each one a link to its own detail page.
    • Click an item in the diagram to fan out its connections in turn. This way you can walk from an account to a business role to the resources that role grants without leaving the page.
    • Click the same circle again, or collapse under the diagram, to fold it back.
    • Drag to move the diagram, scroll to zoom.
    • When a circle holds more items than fit in the diagram, the last one reads +N more in the list below. The list is always complete.
    • Export CSV above the list downloads it.

    Timeline¶

    A line with one dot per day on which something changed, oldest on the left. Click a dot to list that day's changes; the most recent day is selected when you open the tab. Choose how far back to look with 30 days, 90 days, 1 year or All. The tab opens on 90 days.

    Use it to answer "when did this account get that group?" or "who was added to this role last week?". Names in the list are links.

    Changes are recorded from the moment Identity Atlas first read the item. What happened in the source system before that is not on the timeline.

    Diff¶

    Timeline lists events; Diff shows the result. Pick a moment under Compare with an earlier moment (1 day ago, 1 week ago, 30 days ago, 90 days ago, 1 year ago, or a date of your own) and the page puts the item as it was then on the left and as it is Now on the right: every attribute and every relationship, line by line.

    • The chips at the top count what changed, was added, was removed and stayed unchanged.
    • Only show differences hides the unchanged lines.
    • A note appears above the comparison when it cannot tell the whole story, for example when the moment you picked lies before the start of the recorded change history, or when the item did not exist yet.

    Risk¶

    The Risk Assessment panel shows the item's tier, its score from 0 to 100, and when it was scored. Underneath are the four parts the score is built from, each with its own score and its weight in the total:

    Part Weight
    Classifier Match 50%
    Membership Analysis 20%
    Structural / Hygiene 10%
    Risk Propagation 20%

    Show Details opens the reasoning: which classifiers matched and why (Classifier Matches), and the Score Explanation.

    Overriding a score¶

    If you know something the score does not, adjust it:

    1. Click Adjust Score (or Edit Override when there already is one).
    2. Move the Score Adjustment slider, from −50 (lower risk) to +50 (higher risk). The panel shows the resulting score.
    3. Fill in Reason. It is required.
    4. Click Save Override.

    The adjustment is shown next to the score from then on, and the reason is listed under Show Details, so the next reader can see that a person changed the score and why. Remove Override returns the item to its calculated score.

    For what the tiers mean and how a score is calculated, see How Scoring Works.

    What differs per kind¶

    An account¶

    Header: name, the account's type (for example Member, Guest or ServicePrincipal), its sign-in name or e-mail address, the System it lives in, job title, department and company, the Last sign-in when one is known, and any tags.

    Attributes also has an Activity section below the table: the account's most recent sign-ins, with the date each figure was measured. It says No activity recorded when the source system supplied none.

    Relationships circles:

    Circle Contains
    Manager, Direct Reports The account's place in the reporting line
    Contexts The contexts the account is a member of
    Direct, Indirect, Eligible Everything the account holds, split by how it is held: the same three ways as the matrix badges. The list shows the type of each resource, so group memberships, ownerships, application roles and permissions are all here.
    Business Roles The business roles the account holds
    Identity The person this account belongs to

    A few more circles appear only when they apply: Owned Resources (resources this account is the owner of), Owners and Sponsors (who answers for this account, for example for an AI agent or a guest), and the reverse of those, Owned Agents and Sponsored Guests.

    Compare with… in the header puts this account next to one or two others; see Comparing accounts.

    A resource¶

    Header: name, the type of resource (Group, AppRole, and so on), the system it comes from, its description, and any tags.

    The header also names the Owner (or Owners) when one is known. An owner that Identity Atlas holds an account for is a link to that account. An owner that only the source system knows by name is shown as plain text. Hover the name to see how the owner is known.

    Relationships circles:

    Circle Contains
    Direct Members, Indirect Members, Eligible The accounts that hold this resource, split by how
    Business Roles The business roles that grant this resource
    Member Of The resources this one is itself a member of, such as a group inside another group
    Contexts The contexts the resource belongs to
    Owners Who answers for the resource. Only shown when there is an owner.

    This is the page for "who can access this, and why?". Effective Access walks through that question step by step.

    A business role¶

    Header: name, the catalog the role belongs to, its description, its category, and the date and reviewer of the Last Certification when there has been one.

    Attributes has more on it than for the other kinds:

    • Overview, above the table: the role's type, its Review status, the Review date and Reviewed by, and its category.
    • Below the table: Assignment Policies, Access Reviews and Pending Requests, each with a count.

    Relationships circles:

    Circle Contains
    Assignments The accounts that hold the role
    Resources The resources the role grants
    Catalog The catalog the role is part of

    An identity¶

    Header: the person's name, how many accounts they have, the context they belong to (a link), and their job title when the HR source supplies one.

    An identity holds no access itself; its accounts do. So the diagram is small: Linked Accounts and Contexts. To see what the person can do, click an account under Linked Accounts and fan out that account's own circles.

    Below the diagram, the Linked Accounts table lists every account of this person: System, Account, Enabled and Type. The last column says where the link comes from:

    • Linked from source: the source system itself says this account belongs to this person.
    • Confirm and Remove: Identity Atlas matched the account to the person. Confirm the match if it is right, remove it if it is not. Undo takes your decision back.

    How accounts are matched to people is explained in One Person, Many Accounts.

    A context¶

    A context has a page of its own with a different layout. See Contexts.

    Comparing accounts¶

    Diff compares one item at two moments. Compare puts two or three accounts side by side as they are now, to answer "these two people do the same job, so where does their access differ?".

    Open it in either of two ways:

    • Compare with… in an account's header, then add one or two more accounts.
    • On the Principals (Users) tab, select two or three rows and click Compare.

    The page shows one column per account. Only show differences is on to begin with, so you start with just the lines where the accounts differ. Names, e-mail addresses and IDs are hidden as well, because they differ between any two accounts.

    When you arrive through a shared matrix¶

    Someone who opens a shared matrix and clicks a name sees a reduced detail page: the Attributes and Relationships tabs only, with nothing to change. Timeline, Diff and Risk are for analysts.
